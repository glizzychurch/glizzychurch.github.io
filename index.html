THE RAISING

How a pile of lumber became holy ground

In the summer of 2025, four friends came together in the hot sun to raise The Shed from a pile of wood and a box of nails to the glorious place of worship it stands as today.

Give or take a few power tools, a lot of sunscreen, and — for reasons no one has ever fully explained — a dress code of straw hats and suspenders, the whole thing came together in one sweaty weekend. Framing went up, walls went on, and the roof got nailed down plank by plank, one hot afternoon at a time.

By the time the last shingle landed, everyone agreed: this was no longer just a shed.

THE RITES

Two sacraments: the glizzy and the glass

Every gathering here runs on two things — hot dogs rolling all day on Kristoff's roller, and whatever's cold enough to hand you at the bar. Both are treated with appropriate reverence.

The Sacrament

The roller runs from open to close. Toppings are self-serve and taken seriously — mustard is not optional, ketchup is tolerated, and opinions about relish are strong. Devotion is measured in glizzies consumed.

Communion

Mason jars, cold cans, and whatever the cooler's stocked with that day. The bar opens the moment the first car pulls in and doesn't close until someone finally admits it's time to go home.
